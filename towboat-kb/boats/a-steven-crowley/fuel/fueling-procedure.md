# Fueling procedure — A. Steven Crowley

## Before the hose
1. **Close every fill**, including the 3s and 4s that are open for running.
2. Suctions and high suctions: **do not touch.** #4 suctions stay open (engines), high suctions stay open.
3. Open the fills on the tanks you're filling **only**, plus the deck fill valve where the hose lands.

## While fueling
4. Start the dock slow. Confirm only the open tanks are rising. If a closed tank creeps up, a valve
   is passing — stop and find it before taking the full rate.
5. Fill marks: **#2 106", #3/#4/#5 118".** Keep port and starboard even; the hose side runs ahead,
   throttle its fill valve back a little.
6. When a tank nears its mark, **open the next tank's fill BEFORE closing this one.**
7. **Never close the last open fill with the dock pump running** — it deadheads their pump and can
   blow a hose. Signal the dock to slow down before the last tank, close your last fill only after
   they've stopped.
8. Hose stops → blow down → disconnect → shut the deck fill valve.

## After
9. Set the running lineup: fills open on the 3s and 4s (or whatever lineup the chief wants), all
   other fills closed.
10. Sound all eight tanks for the log after they settle. Sound the 2s and 5s once to confirm they
    took nothing.
11. Fuel log: enter received gallons; soundings are the record, the purchase math is the estimate.

## Worked example — 2026-10-23
- Before: 72,330 est. on hand, 754 est. used for the day. Received **10,009 gal** into the 3s then
  the 4s (4s not topped). Water 9,500 − 500 used + 6,000 received = 15,000.
- After settling, 3s and 4s gravitated together to ~91": soundings 2s 103", 3s 91", 4s 91", 5s 115"
  both sides → **82,198 gal** by the tables, **895 used**. (91.5" would be 82,388 / 705 — within sounding accuracy of the estimate.)
