# Steering — rudder room and pumps

## Layout
- **Rudder room** (all the way aft, below deck): steering rudder stocks come up through the deck
  with heavy tiller arms, rams on pedestals, tie bars linking port/stbd pairs, rudder stops at hard
  over. Flanking gear is the same idea forward of the wheels, with its own rams and control valve.
- **Steering rudders** (behind the wheels): steer going ahead. **Flanking rudders** (two ahead of
  each wheel): steer while backing. Separate levers in the pilothouse.
- **Feedback link:** short bracket + vertical rod on top of each steering tiller, driving a thin rod
  aft to the control valve. This is the "you're there, stop" signal and the calibration of zero.
  Photo with it pointed out: `/docs/steering-feedback-link-photo.jpg`.
- **Steering pumps:** two electric motor/pump sets on one reservoir in the engine room, sight glass
  on the tank, two gauges on top. One runs, one is spare. **Swapped every turnaround** so both wear
  evenly and the spare is proved.

## Rounds
- Oil level in the sight glass; oil on the deck under rams and pumps.
- Keepers on every ram and tie bar pin. Bolts/keys where tiller meets stock.
- Packing gland weeping vs streaming.
- Pump noise, heat, breaker, gauge reading.
- Rudder angle indicator agrees with the pilot.

## Dock check for "boat pulls one way"
Pumps on, pilothouse at zero: measure each steering tiller to its stop, both sides — should match.
Same for ram rod extension. Check flanking rudders centered. Wiggle feedback ball joints (pumps off,
tagged). Watch rams for creep. **2026-10-26: all of this measured dead even** — and the boat still
pulled to port, because the starboard rudder wasn't following its stock in the water. See the issue file.

## Safety
Everything swings with no warning and the pilot can't see you. No work in the swing area without
the pilothouse told, pumps off and tagged, engineer's OK. Hydraulic oil is >1,000 psi — find leaks
with cardboard, not hands.
