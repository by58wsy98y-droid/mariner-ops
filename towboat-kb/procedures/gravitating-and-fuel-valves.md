# Gravitating tanks

## The rule
Gravity only moves fuel through a connection that's **under the fuel on both ends.** Fuel can't
climb up a pipe to get into the next tank. So the gravity path is whichever header sits low:

- **Standard towboat plumbing:** suctions come off the bottom → open **suction to suction**
  (same side pair, receiving tank first, no other suctions on the header). Fill lines come in at the
  top → fills are a dead header until something pumps into it.
- **Boats where the fill header runs low** (A. Steven Crowley is one): open **fill to fill**. The
  fills are the burn-down lineup; suctions are for pumps and emergencies.

**Don't guess.** Open the pair, sound both tanks over an hour. Moving = that's the gravity path.

## What each valve is for
| Valve | Opens when… |
|---|---|
| Suction | Fuel is leaving the tank: to the engines, to the transfer pump, over the side, stripping |
| Fill | Fuel is being pushed INTO the tank: dock, transfer pump discharge, centrifuge return — or gravity, on fill-header boats |
| High suction | Centrifuge/polisher pickup above the sludge line (usually always open) |

## Habits that keep you out of trouble
- Open the receiving tank first, then the giving tank.
- Keep it to same-side pairs — cross-connecting port and starboard lets fuel run to the low side on a list.
- Fill valves shut on every tank you're not filling. Anything pumping into the fill header goes everywhere that's open.
- Gravitating only **equalizes** levels. Topping off is a pump job.
- Know which pickups are always open on your boat (engine supply, centrifuge) — tanks on a shared open header talk to each other whether you like it or not.

## Where this came from
Worked out on the A. Steven Crowley, October 2026 — the chief's fueling sheet showed the fills were
the gravity path there, the opposite of the textbook answer.
