# Starboard steering rudder not turning in the water

## Symptoms
- Boat swings to port. Not noticeable with 5 barges; obvious with 15 loads (all loads, made up square).
- Pilot: "a hell of an effort to go starboard, swings port with the slightest turn of the sticks."
- **With the rudders turned, the port wheel wash deflects; the starboard wheel wash keeps going straight.**

## Checks done
| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Tiller-to-stop, both steering rudders, pilothouse at zero, tied up | **Even, on the dot** |
| Feedback link on the steering tiller (bracket looked bent in photo) | Inspected; not the cause |
| Tow makeup | All loads, square — not a makeup problem |
| Wash observation | Starboard rudder not turning the wash → rudder not following its stock |

## Diagnosis
Starboard steering rudder is disconnected from what moves on deck. Port rudder is steering the boat
alone; a dead rudder sitting cocked = constant push to one side, which is exactly the symptom.

## Next step (dock, pumps on)
Scribe/paint a line across the starboard tiller hub, nut and stock collar. Swing hard over and back.
- **Line broken** → tiller turned, stock didn't → sheared key / loose hub. Above-deck repair.
- **Line intact** → stock turned, rudder didn't → coupling, stock below the bearing, or the blade. Diver, likely dry dock.
Also: watch both flanking rudders' wash while backing to confirm flanking control is intact.
