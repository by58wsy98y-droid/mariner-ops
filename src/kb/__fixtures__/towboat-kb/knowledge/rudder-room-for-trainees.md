# The Rudder Room

The compartment all the way aft, under the deck, where the rudders come up through the bottom of the
boat. The pilot moves a lever; everything that makes the rudders answer it is in this room.

## The parts, from the water up
- **Rudders** — doors hanging in the water. **Steering rudders** sit behind the propellers and steer
  going ahead. **Flanking rudders** sit in front (two per wheel) and steer while backing — that's how a
  pilot slides a tow down through a bend he can't drive through.
- **Rudder stock** — the post each rudder hangs on, up through the hull into the room. A few drops
  from the packing gland is normal; a stream is not.
- **Tiller arm** — the lever on top of the stock. Push the arm, the rudder turns.
- **Tie bar** — links a pair of tiller arms so both rudders move together, like a tie rod on a truck.
- **Rams** — hydraulic cylinders that push the tiller arms. Steering rams and flanking rams are separate.
- **Pumps and tank** — two electric pumps, one reservoir with a sight glass, gauges. Pumps are the heart, oil is the blood.
- **Rudder stops** — steel blocks the tiller hits at hard over.
- **Feedback rod** — thin linkage on the tiller that tells the valve "you're there, stop" and sends the
  true angle to the pilothouse gauge.

## The two steering pumps
Only one runs. We swap every turnaround so both wear evenly and the spare is proved every trip. The
pilothouse always knows before a pump gets switched. Won't start, sounds different, trips = report now.

## Pinch points — this room can kill you
- The swing of the tiller arms, tie bars and rams. Never step over a tie bar. Never put a hand on a pin, a rod, or an arm.
- Between an arm and a stop block at hard over.
- Hydraulic lines over 1,000 psi — a pinhole leak goes through skin. Cardboard, never a hand.
- **Rule:** no work in the swing area until the pilothouse is told, pumps are off and tagged, and the
  engineer says go. The river can still push a rudder with the pumps off.

## Rounds
Oil on the deck or on a ram · sight glass dropping · keepers on every pin · loose bolts / shiny key
where the arm meets the stock · play in tie bar ends (pumps off, tagged) · glands · pump noise, heat,
breaker, gauge · filter indicator · alarms · rudder angle vs the gauge · where emergency steering is.

## Before signing off
1. Which rudders steer ahead, which steer backing?
2. What does the tie bar do?
3. Why swap pumps every turnaround?
4. Three places in here that can pinch or crush you?
5. Fresh oil under a ram on your round — what do you do?
6. Sight glass lower than yesterday — what do you do?
